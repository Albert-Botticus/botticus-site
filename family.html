Every school portal in one place

PowerSchool, ParentVue, ClassDojo, Canvas, Google Classroom, the lunch account, the district email blast. The Bot logs into all of them and sends one morning digest: grade changed, assignment missing, slip due Friday, picture day, lunch balance low. A calendar that routes itself

Practices, games, rehearsals, early dismissals, the orthodontist. Pulled from TeamSnap, GameChanger, school calendars and emails, then pushed to the right person's calendar with the right lead time. The kid gets the 30-minute reminder. You get the "who's driving" question the night before, not the morning of.

Teens can talk to it

From 16, a teenager can have their own Telegram thread, set up by a parent. "When's practice?" "What's for dinner?" "Tell Mom I'll be late." The Bot answers, relays, and logs. You see everything. Teen mode means no purchases, no outside contacts, no open internet, and a fixed list of things it will do. Younger kids don't talk to the Bot at all; it runs their logistics through you.

Kids can have a site

Lemonade stand, lawn mowing, baking, the robotics team, a portfolio. You ask, the Bot builds a real page in minutes, you approve it before it goes anywhere, and it lives on your family's own site. First name only, no photos of the kid, no school, no address. A first business with training wheels.

Kids and teens, safely

Who talks to the Bot, and who doesn't.

This is the part we are strictest about, and the rules below are not optional extras. They are how every family Bot is built.

Your accounts, your rules, your eyes

  • Children under 16 never interact with the Bot. It handles their schedules, portals and reminders through you, the parent.
  • From 16, a parent can set up a teen's Telegram thread, where Telegram's own age rules allow it. Only accounts you approve can reach the Bot.
  • Every conversation with a teen is visible to the parents. Nothing is private from you.
  • Teen mode is a fixed list: schedule, reminders, relaying messages to family, homework nudges, chores. No purchases, no new contacts, no browsing, no money, no exceptions they can talk it into.
  • The Bot says it is a Bot. Every teen's thread opens with that, in plain words.

What we promise about their data

  • The Bot runs inside accounts your family owns. Botticus does not collect, store or sell information about your children. Anything the Bot knows about a child came from a parent.
  • Where a teen under 18 can talk to a Bot, it runs on the AI provider's commercial platform with its guidance for users under 18 applied: content filtering, monitoring, and parental oversight, in line with the provider's guidelines for organizations whose products interact with minors and with children's privacy law, including COPPA.
  • A child's website carries a first name only. No photos of the child, no school, no address, no last name. A parent approves it before it goes live, and can take it down with one message.
  • You can read, change or delete every rule and every memory. They are files you own.
