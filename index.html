The idea

Not a chatbot. A chief of staff.

A generic AI assistant answers questions. A Bot from Botticus does the work, inside your email, your spreadsheets, your ordering portals, your inventory, your calendar. It writes the email, builds the order, updates the sheet, and chases the invoice. It knows your customers, your rules, your pricing, and your tone, because it was built around them.

01

Tailored to you

Your bot gets a name, a personality, and a rulebook written from how you actually work. Mine is called Albert. Yours might be Pip, Marge, or Sarge. It learns your business, not "a" business.

02

Always on

It works on a schedule you never think about: every 5 minutes, every morning at 6, every Monday. Orders come in at 2 AM? Handled. Customer asks a question on Sunday? Drafted and waiting.

03

Yours, not theirs

It runs in accounts you own, using systems you already pay for. There is no new platform to move your life into, and nobody else's AI training on your customers.

Let's talk
Questions

The things people ask first.

Is this just ChatGPT with a nickname?

No. A chat window answers questions. A Bot is an agent with memory, a rulebook, scheduled jobs, and connections into your real systems. It uses leading AI models to think, and then it acts: sends, builds, posts, reconciles. The model is the engine. The Bot is the car, built for your roads.

Can it really send emails and build orders on its own?

Yes, once you let it. Every bot starts in draft-and-approve mode, where you see everything before it goes out. Then you decide, rule by rule, what it may do unattended. Albert sends carrier notices automatically but never emails certain customers without a human. Your rules will be different, and they'll be yours.

What happens to my data?

Your bot runs against accounts you own: your email, your Google or Microsoft workspace, your marketplaces, your portals. We don't move your business onto a platform we control, and we don't train anything on your customers. You can revoke access at any time.

Do I need to be technical?

No. If you can text, you can run a Bot. The technical part is ours, which is exactly why there's a monthly care plan.

What if it breaks?

It will, occasionally, the same way any system does. The care plan covers monitoring, fixes, and a person you can reach, not a help article.

Why not just use Muse, ChatGPT Dots or Grok Bot?

If you want a helper for your personal life, you might. All three are built around one account, run on Meta's, OpenAI's or xAI's computers, and connect to what's in their catalogs. A Bot is built for a business: it runs where your systems run, it works for your team, its routine jobs are scripted so they behave the same every time, and its rules and memory are files you can read. And when something breaks, you call the person who built it.

What does it cost?

A one-time setup fee plus a monthly care plan. The numbers depend on how many systems the bot touches and how much it's trusted to do alone, so we quote after the discovery call. The call is free and there's no pressure.
