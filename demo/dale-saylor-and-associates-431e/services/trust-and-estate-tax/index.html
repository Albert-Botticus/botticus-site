Income tax, estate tax and gift tax

We prepare the income tax returns for estates and many types of trusts, including GRATs and CRUTs. We also prepare estate and gift tax returns.

That includes estate tax returns filed to preserve the Deceased Spousal Unused Exclusion. Filing for that purpose can matter even when no estate tax is due.

Two of our CPAs, Josh Stefanko and Cory Johnson, have direct experience with estate and trust income tax preparation, and Cory also with CRUT returns.

What to bring

  • Death certificate, for an estate
  • The will or trust document
  • Account statements and valuations as of the relevant date
  • Records of income received and expenses paid by the estate or trust
  • Prior gift tax returns, if any
  • Contact details for the executor, trustee or attorney

Questions about trust and estate returns

Does an estate file its own income tax return?

Often, yes. An estate or trust can be a separate taxpayer and may need its own income tax return. We can tell you what applies to your situation.

What is the Deceased Spousal Unused Exclusion?

It lets a surviving spouse use the unused part of a deceased spouse's estate tax exclusion. An estate tax return is filed to preserve it.
